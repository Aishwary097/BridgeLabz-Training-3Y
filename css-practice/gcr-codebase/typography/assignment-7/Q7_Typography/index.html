The language of type

Good type
makes room
for meaning.

A short article on the quiet choices behind readable words.

Reading is a rhythm.

The shape of a sentence changes when the space around it changes. A clear line invites us in; a crowded one asks us to work before the story even begins.

On a page, type is more than a container for words. Its weight can bring a thought forward, while a gentle emphasis lets another note sit quietly beside it. A generous line-height gives the eye room to travel. Thoughtful letter-spacing can make a small label feel considered without making the paragraph harder to read.

“Readability is a form of welcome.”

Size matters, too. A large heading sets the pace. Smaller supporting text gives the main idea space to land. When these decisions work together, a page feels effortless, even though every detail has been chosen.

Good typography does not compete with the idea. It helps the idea arrive.
